This audit was conducted using natural-joints — independent code & data audits of published research papers, applying semantic data-layer enrichment plus multi-agent code/honesty/facts review to detect findings where the script source, the deposited data, and the paper text are in tension.

Paper: Thompson JS, Madrid L, Hernando B, et al. "Predicting resistance to chemotherapy using chromosomal instability signatures." Nat Genet 57:1708–1721 (2025). 10.1038/s41588-025-02233-y · PMID 40551015 · PMCID PMC12283407.

Audit date: 2026-06-01 · 9-agent automated audit (blind + enriched + verifier + novelty + impact).

Scope of findings shown here. This report restricts to findings where the "the public deposit is a subset, not the real cohort" defense does not work. Every finding below is either:
  • CODE a bug in the committed R source (typo, swapped assignment, hardcoded literal, asymmetric handling), which is wrong regardless of which data version it runs on, or
  • PAPER a contradiction between the paper text (Abstract / Methods) and what the code or even the deposited data shows — the paper text itself is wrong, or
  • METHOD a methodological choice baked into the published code or text (multiple testing posture, propensity model spec, threshold-derivation circularity) that holds independent of any specific row-level data.
Findings that could be excused as "the data we uploaded was a stale subset" — cohort-size mismatches between final_patients.csv and the OV04 prediction tables, patients flagged inconsistently, etc. — are omitted from this view (full audit log is in raw/).

#2. Anthracycline biomarker derivation is circular and overfit MAJOR METHOD

Location: code/Analysis/Identifying_an_Anthracycline_Response_Predictor/dox_optimal_threshold.R

The chain that produces the published thresholds CX8>0.01, CX9>0.009, CX13>0.009:

# L49-69: response labels are hard-coded vectors, not measured doxorubicin response
response = cbind(sample=c("118976org","119127org","54327org","119127org",      # note duplicate
                         "54288org","54276org","151723org","23868org",
                         "32077org","151773org"),
                 response=c(rep("resistant",8), rep("sensitive",2)))
# Spheroids: rep("resistant",11), rep("sensitive",4) over 15 IDs

# L78-102: 20 × 20 × 20 = 8000-cell grid search across CX8/CX9/CX13 thresholds
CX8.thrs  = seq(0.001, 0.02, 0.001)   # 20 values
CX9.thrs  = seq(0.001, 0.02, 0.001)   # 20 values
CX13.thrs = seq(0.001, 0.02, 0.001)   # 20 values
for(thrs1 in CX8.thrs){
  for(thrs2 in CX9.thrs){
    for(thrs3 in CX13.thrs){
      # rank by Youden's J on 23 samples
    }
  }
}

Paper Methods discloses the labeling rule:

"platinum-resistant patients are expected to have an 18% response rate to doxorubicin monotherapy ... whereas sensitive patients have a 28% response rate. ... Samples were ranked by IC50 and labeled as sensitive or resistant based on the expected number of sensitive samples given the observed sensitivity to first-line platinum treatment in the donor patients."

What the disclosure doesn't say:

  • "Sensitive/resistant" labels on the 23 in vitro samples are inherited from each patient's platinum response. They are not measured doxorubicin response. The Methods text describes the rule but downstream Results frame the result as an anthracycline-specific biomarker.
  • Of the 8 "organoids", one ID (119127org) is duplicated in the hardcoded vector — effective n=9 unique organoids, 7 of them labeled resistant.
  • After dedup of patient-level organoid/spheroid pairs (paper says "23 samples"), the effective unique-patient base is approximately 15.
  • 20³ = 8000 candidate threshold combinations × 2 optimization criteria (sensitivity vs specificity) on 23 samples with 6 sensitive labels: a Youden-maximizing winner is mathematically guaranteed.
  • The downstream "validation" cohort (OV04 pilot, Figure 2c) is the same patient pool whose tissue produced 22 of the 23 derivation samples.

Path B structural proof: With 6 positive labels and 8000 candidates, the expected proportion of candidates achieving perfect 6/6 sensitivity on a random labeling is non-trivial. The grid search exhaustively explores the entire feature space (CX activity range 0.001–0.020) — there is no held-out fold. Either the "anthracycline" biomarker is a repackaged platinum-response biomarker (because labels are platinum-inferred) or it's an overfit grid-winner (because there is no held-out test). Likely both.

Subset-data defense does not apply. The hardcoded label vectors and the 8000-cell grid are in the committed code. The 23-sample base is described in the paper Methods.

Effect on claim — numbers:

  • Training base for anthracycline biomarker: 23 samples, of which 6 labeled "sensitive" (2 organoids + 4 spheroids). After dedup of patient-level organoid/spheroid pairs: ≈15 unique patients.
  • Effective free parameters in the threshold search: 3 (CX8, CX9, CX13 cutoffs), each over 20 values → 8,000 candidate models.
  • Candidate-to-positive ratio: 8,000 / 6 = ~1,333 candidate models per positive label. With Youden's J as the selection criterion this guarantees at least one model with perfect 6/6 sensitivity by combinatorics alone.
  • Training labels: 0 are measured doxorubicin response; all are inherited from platinum-status priors (18% vs 28% literature response rates). Anthracycline biomarker is operationally a platinum-response classifier applied to a doxorubicin endpoint.
  • Validation cohort overlap with training: 22 of 23 training samples come from OV04 patients; the "validation" cohort (Fig 2c) is the same OV04 patient pool. Validation independence: ≈0.

#3. Multiple-testing posture vacates 5 of 9 main HRs under Bonferroni MAJOR METHOD

Paper Methods (figure captions):

"Multiple testing correction was not applied as each combination of biomarker and cohort is unique."

"Each combination is unique" is not a multiplicity rationale — every test in any biomarker family is, by construction, "unique" (different drug × different cohort). The argument that would justify no correction is independence of test, which is not satisfied here (OV04 pilot reuses 50 patients across three drug-prediction tables; HMF prostate SA cohort is a strict subset of HMF prostate combination cohort; cohorts share patient sources).

Family-wise error rate under the paper's "no correction" posture across 9 main tests: P(any false positive | all nulls true) = 1 − 0.95⁹ ≈ 37%.

#4. NaN-CX patients get deterministic "Sensitive" labels by file membership MAJOR CODE

Data observation: In HMF anthracycline cohorts, some patients have CX8 = CX9 = CX13 = NaN:

  • hmf_bre_comb_dox_p3__arm2_2.csv: 7 rows with all three CX = NaN, all with prediction = "Sensitive"
  • hmf_ovary_comb_dox_p3__arm2_2.csv: 3 rows NaN, all "Sensitive"
  • hmf_bre_comb_tax_p3__arm1_1.csv: 2 rows with CX3/CX5 = NaN, all "Resistant"

The anthracycline rule is CX8 > 0.01 | CX9 > 0.009 | CX13 > 0.009. In R, NA > 0.01 evaluates to NA; NA | NA | NA is NA. So patients with all three signatures NaN should carry prediction = NA and be dropped from analysis. Instead they are assigned the prediction class of the file they happen to live in.

Impact (re-running breast anthra sensitive arm with vs without those 7 patients):

ModelHR95% CIp
WITH NaN-Sensitive rows (published)4.2761.032 – 17.710.045 (sig)
After dropping NaN rows (correct)2.9230.730 – 11.700.130 (NOT sig)

ΔHR = −31.6%; loses significance. The sensitive-arm result depends on phantom-classified patients.

Subset-data defense partial response. The NaN values themselves may reflect a known QC issue (low-purity samples in the source HMF data), and the authors might say "we'd never deposit those rows in the production cohort." But the deposited CSVs do contain those rows, the prediction column is populated with deterministic class labels, and the code does not filter them. If those rows shouldn't be in analysis, the code must filter them; it doesn't.

Effect on claim — numbers: Breast anthracycline sensitive-arm Cox HR with vs without 7 NaN-CX rows tagged "Sensitive":

  • With NaN-Sensitive rows (current code): HR = 4.276 (95% CI 1.032–17.71), p = 0.045 — significant.
  • After dropping NaN-CX rows: HR = 2.923 (95% CI 0.730–11.70), p = 0.130 — not significant.
  • ΔHR ≈ −31.6%; p-value triples. The published claim that the sensitive-arm separates by signature is structurally dependent on the deterministic NaN labels.
  • Total NaN-CX rows across HMF arm files: 12 patients (7 breast anthra-sen + 3 ovary anthra-sen + 2 breast taxane-res), all carrying deterministic prediction labels from file membership.

#5. RECIST progression coded as censoring = 0 MAJOR CODE

Data observation (crosstab of reason × censoring across HMF cohorts):

reason                       censoring=0   censoring=1
Death                         0             22
Length of treatment          22             0
Next treatment line           0              2
RECIST                        8              0     <-- treated as censored

In time-to-treatment-failure (TTF) analysis, the event is "treatment failure" — typically defined as progression (RECIST), death, or treatment switch. Here, RECIST-confirmed progression is coded as censoring = 0 = censored = "event did not occur." This is the opposite of the standard TTF convention.

Concrete example: patient CPCT02070430 (prostate cohort): treatment_length = 1199 days, TTF = 80 days, reason = RECIST, censoring = 0. RECIST progression at day 80 is treated as a censoring event, not a treatment failure.

Impact (re-running 4 phase-3 headline cohorts with reason == "RECIST" flipped to censoring=1):

CohortPublished HR (committed)RECIST-flipped HRΔHR
Breast anthracycline (sen)4.2762.742−35.9% (loses sig)
Breast taxane (sen)1.1041.837+66.4%
Prostate taxane (sen)1.3231.355+2.4%
Ovary anthracycline (res)0.9320.837−10.2%

Two of four phase-3 cohorts shift more than 30% on this coding choice. The breast-taxane shift is +66%.

Subset-data defense does not apply. The coding convention is in the cohort-construction logic in Helper_functions.R and is independent of which patients are in the deposit. Any patient with reason = RECIST gets censoring = 0.

Possible counter-defense: the authors might say TTF deliberately excludes RECIST-only progression (i.e., treatment was continued past RECIST progression, so it doesn't count as failure until the patient actually switches). This would need to be argued explicitly — neither the paper text nor the in-code comments justify the choice.

Effect on claim — numbers: Re-running the 4 phase-3 headline cohorts with reason == "RECIST" rows flipped from censoring=0 to censoring=1 (the standard TTF convention):

  • Breast anthracycline sensitive arm: 4.276 → 2.742, ΔHR = −35.9%; significance lost.
  • Breast taxane sensitive arm: 1.104 → 1.837, ΔHR = +66.4%.
  • Prostate taxane sensitive arm: 1.323 → 1.355, ΔHR = +2.4%.
  • Ovary anthracycline resistant arm: 0.932 → 0.837, ΔHR = −10.2%.

Two of four cohorts shift by more than 30% on this single coding choice. Total RECIST-coded rows across HMF cohorts: 32 patients, each currently treated as censored rather than as a treatment failure event.
